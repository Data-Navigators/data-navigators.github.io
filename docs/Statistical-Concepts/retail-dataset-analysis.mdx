# **Exercise**: Retail Dataset Analysis & Problem Solving 📦🛒

In this module, we will introduce a **Retail Sales Dataset** and pinch your curiousity on problem-solving exercises that will help you understand key statistical concepts like **descriptive statistics, distributions,EDA and visualizations**.

## Retail Dataset Overview 🗂

The **Retail Dataset** contains information about sales transactions, customers, and products. Let’s start by getting familiar with the columns in the dataset and understanding what they represent.

### Columns in the Dataset:

1. **Customer ID** 🔑
   - A unique identifier for each customer in the dataset.
  
2. **Gender** 👩‍🦰👨‍🦱
   - The gender of the customer (Male or Female).
  
3. **Age** 🎂
   - The age of the customer.
  
4. **Product Category** 🛍️
   - The category to which the purchased product belongs (e.g., Electronics, Clothing, Home Appliances, etc.).
  
5. **Quantity** 🛒
   - The number of units of the product purchased in the transaction.
  
6. **Price per Unit** 💵
   - The price of a single unit of the product purchased.
  
7. **Total Amount** 💰
   - The total amount spent by the customer for the transaction, calculated as `Quantity * Price per Unit`.

This exercise is designed to take approximately 1 to 1.5 hours and is intended to test a candidate's ability to move from raw data exploration to statistical reasoning and a business-ready conclusion.

Use the sample dataset below as the working data. The goal is not to find a single textbook answer, but to build a coherent analytical story using descriptive statistics, EDA, and hypothesis testing.

### Exercise Questions

1. **Data quality and descriptive summary**
   - Review the dataset for missing values, inconsistent entries, and any obvious data issues.
   - Compute a concise summary of the main numerical variables: Age, Quantity, Price per Unit, and Total Amount.
   - Explain what these summary measures suggest about the retail mix and customer spending pattern.

2. **Exploratory Data Analysis: customer and product patterns**
   - Build a profile of the customer base using age, gender, and product category distribution.
   - Compare category-level spending patterns and identify which categories contribute the most revenue and which appear relatively low-value.
   - Create at least two visuals that help explain the customer and product patterns. Interpret them in business language rather than just describing the chart.

3. **Relationship and outlier analysis**
   - Investigate whether there is a relationship between age and total spend, and whether that relationship is different across product categories.
   - Identify any outliers or unusually high-value transactions and discuss whether they are likely to be genuine business signals or potential data anomalies.
   - Explain how such observations might affect the decision to rely on averages alone.

4. **Formulate and test a business hypothesis**
   - Frame a business question using the same dataset, for example: whether spending differs by gender or whether one product category has a materially different customer profile.
   - Choose an appropriate statistical test based on the type of variable and the research question.
   - State the null and alternative hypotheses clearly, explain whether the test conditions are reasonable for this dataset, and interpret the outcome in business terms.

5. **Decision-oriented conclusion**
   - Write a short business recommendation based on your findings from the descriptive analysis and hypothesis testing.
   - Discuss whether the evidence is strong enough to support a strategy change, and what additional information would make the conclusion more reliable.
   - Contrast the risk of a false positive and a false negative in this retail setting, and explain which type of mistake would be more costly for the business.

### What this exercise is testing

This task is deliberately designed to require a candidate to:

* inspect the data before applying analysis,
* choose appropriate visuals and summary statistics,
* connect patterns to actual business meaning,
* decide which statistical method matches the question,
* interpret uncertainty and decision risk,
* and present a conclusion that is evidence-based rather than formula-driven.

A strong answer should be a short analytical report, not a list of raw outputs. It should show reasoning, assumptions, and judgment.
