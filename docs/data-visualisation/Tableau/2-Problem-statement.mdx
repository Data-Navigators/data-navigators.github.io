# Business Problem and Data Preparation

In the previous section, we introduced the Travel Insurance dataset and how Tableau fits into the analytical workflow.

Before creating visualisations, we first need to:

1. understand the business problem,
2. translate it into analytical questions,
3. review the available data,
4. prepare the data for analysis,
5. define the business metrics we will use.

The objective is not simply:

> **"Create a travel insurance dashboard."**

Instead, we need to ask:

> **"What does the business need to know, and what decision should our analysis support?"**

---

# Business Context

Consider a company that facilitates the sale of travel insurance through different agencies and distribution channels.

The company offers different travel insurance products for customers travelling to destinations around the world.

Management wants to understand how different parts of the portfolio are performing and where potential claim risk may exist.

---

# Business Problem

Management asks:

> **Which travel insurance products, channels and regions are performing well, and where should the company focus its attention to support sustainable business performance while managing claim risk?**

---

# Business Decision

The analysis should help management understand:

- which products contribute most to the business,
- which distribution channels perform strongly,
- which regions contribute most to sales,
- where claim risk may be higher,
- which areas may require further investigation.

---

# Analytical Questions

We can translate the business problem into a small number of analytical questions.

## Overall Performance

- How many policies have been sold?
- How much Net Sales has been generated?
- What is the overall Net Profit?
- What is the overall Profit Margin?
- What is the overall Claim Rate?

## Product Performance

- Which products contribute most to Net Sales?
- Are sales concentrated in a small number of products?

## Channel Performance

- Which distribution channels contribute most to the business?
- How does performance differ across channels?

## Regional Performance

- Which regions contribute most to Net Sales?
- How do Claim Rates differ across regions?
- Are there regions that may require closer attention?

These questions will guide the Tableau analysis later.

---

# The Raw Dataset

We will use the public Travel Insurance dataset from Kaggle:

[Travel Insurance Dataset - Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mhdzahier/travel-insurance)

The original dataset contains 11 fields.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Agency | Name of travel insurance agency |
| Agency Type | Type of agency |
| Distribution Channel | Distribution channel |
| Product Name | Travel insurance product |
| Claim | Claim status: Yes / No |
| Duration | Length of travel |
| Destination | Travel destination |
| Net Sales | Value of travel insurance sales |
| Commission | Commission value |
| Gender | Gender of insured traveller |
| Age | Age of insured traveller |

Before analysing the data in Tableau, we will perform several simple preparation steps.

---

# Why Prepare the Data?

Raw datasets are rarely ready for analysis immediately.

Common issues may include:

- unclear field names,
- missing values,
- inconsistent category labels,
- fields that need to be grouped,
- business metrics that do not yet exist.

Data preparation helps make the dataset easier to understand and analyse consistently.

---

# Data Preparation Exercise

Keep the original downloaded dataset unchanged.

Create a separate prepared version, for example:

`travel_insurance_prepared.csv`

Your workflow should be:

**Raw Dataset → Prepare Data → Save Prepared Dataset → Tableau**

---

# Step 1: Review the Data

Open the raw dataset and inspect the columns.

Look for:

- missing values,
- inconsistent text values,
- unusual values,
- negative Net Sales,
- inconsistent destination names.

Do not automatically remove unusual records.

First ask:

> **Could this value have a legitimate business meaning?**

For example, negative Net Sales may represent refunds, cancellations or reversals.

---

# Step 2: Rename Fields

Rename:

`Distribution Channel`

to:

`Channel`

This makes the field shorter and easier to use in Tableau.

You may also rename the Commission field to:

`Commission (S$)`

if the values represent Singapore dollars.

---

# Step 3: Handle Missing Gender Values

Review the `Gender` field.

If blank values exist, replace them with:

`N/A`

For example:

```text
M = Male
F = Female
N/A = Not Available
```

This allows the records to remain in the dataset while keeping missing information visible.

---

# Step 4: Standardise Destination Names

Review the unique values in:

`Destination`

Look for:

- inconsistent country names,
- unusually long names,
- different labels referring to the same place.

A simple workflow is:

1. create a list of unique Destination values,
2. review inconsistent names,
3. define a standard naming convention,
4. replace inconsistent labels.

For example:

```text
KOREA, REPUBLIC OF → SOUTH KOREA
VIET NAM → VIETNAM
BRUNEI DARUSSALAM → BRUNEI
RUSSIAN FEDERATION → RUSSIA
```

Keep the mapping so that the transformation can be reviewed later.

---

# Step 5: Add Region

To support higher-level analysis, add a new field called:

`Region`

Map each Destination into a broader region.

For example:

| Destination | Region |
|---|---|
| Singapore | Asia |
| Malaysia | Asia |
| Japan | Asia |
| France | Europe |
| Australia | Oceania |
| United States | Americas |

Create a separate mapping table:

**Destination → Region**

You can then use a lookup function such as `VLOOKUP` or `XLOOKUP` to populate the Region field.

This allows the analysis to move from:

**Overall Portfolio → Region → Destination**

For this exercise, Region is sufficient. You do not need to create a more detailed Sub-Region classification.

---

# Step 6: Create Claim Flag

The original `Claim` field contains:

- Yes
- No

Create a new numerical field called:

`Claim Flag`

Use:

```text
Yes = 1
No = 0
```

You may create this in Excel or directly in Tableau.

In Tableau:

```text
IF [Claim] = "Yes" THEN 1
ELSE 0
END
```

This allows Claim Rate to be calculated later.

---

# Step 7: Create Net Profit

For this teaching exercise, use the following definition:

```text
Net Profit = Net Sales - Commission
```

You may create this field in Excel or Tableau.

In Tableau:

```text
[Net Sales] - [Commission (S$)]
```

Name the field:

`Net Profit`

---

# Step 8: Create Profit Margin

Create:

```text
IF [Net Sales] = 0 THEN 0
ELSE [Net Profit] / [Net Sales]
END
```

Name the field:

`Profit Margin`

Format the result as a percentage.

---

# Important Note About Metric Definitions

For this exercise, we are using:

```text
Net Profit = Net Sales - Commission
```

and:

```text
Profit Margin = Net Profit / Net Sales
```

These definitions are used to support the teaching exercise and sample executive dashboard.

In a real organisation, business metric definitions must be confirmed with stakeholders.

A true accounting profitability calculation may require additional information such as:

- claim payout amounts,
- operating expenses,
- acquisition costs,
- servicing costs,
- other business expenses.

These fields are not available in the original dataset.

The important lesson is:

> **Always understand and validate what a business metric represents before using it for decision making.**

---

# Define the Business Metrics

Before building any visualisations, define the key metrics clearly.

| Metric | Definition | Business Question |
|---|---|---|
| Policies Sold | Count of policy records | How much policy volume do we have? |
| Total Net Sales | Sum of Net Sales | How much sales value was generated? |
| Total Net Profit | Sum of Net Profit | What is the reference profitability measure? |
| Profit Margin* | Net Profit / Net Sales | How much reference profit is generated relative to sales? |
| Claim Rate | Average Claim Flag | What proportion of policies result in claims? |

`*` The profitability definitions are used for this exercise and should be validated against actual business definitions in a real organisation.

---

# Analytical Plan

We can now connect the business questions to the data we need.

| Analytical Question | Metric | Dimension |
|---|---|---|
| What is the overall performance? | Policies, Net Sales, Net Profit, Profit Margin, Claim Rate | Overall |
| Which products contribute most? | Net Sales | Product Name |
| Which channels contribute most? | Net Sales | Channel |
| Which regions contribute most? | Net Sales | Region |
| Which regions may require closer attention? | Claim Rate | Region |

Notice that we still have not started creating charts.

First, we have defined:

- the business problem,
- the questions,
- the data,
- the required preparation,
- the metrics.
