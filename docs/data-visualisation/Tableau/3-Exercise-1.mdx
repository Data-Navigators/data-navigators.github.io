# Analyse and Visualise the Data

You should now have a prepared version of the Travel Insurance dataset.

Before reaching this stage, we have already:

1. understood the business problem,
2. translated the problem into analytical questions,
3. reviewed and prepared the raw data,
4. created the required analytical fields,
5. defined the key business metrics.

We can now begin analysing the data in Tableau.

For every visualisation, remember:

> **Business Question → Metric + Dimension → Visualisation → Insight**

The purpose of this exercise is not to create as many charts as possible.

Instead, we will create a small number of useful views that can later be combined into an executive dashboard.

---

# Step 1: Connect the Prepared Dataset

1. Open Tableau Public.
2. Select **Text File** or the appropriate file connection.
3. Open your prepared dataset:

`travel_insurance_prepared.csv`

4. Review the fields and data types.
5. Check that Tableau has correctly identified the dimensions and measures.

---

# Step 2: Create Any Missing Calculated Fields

If you did not create these fields during data preparation, create them in Tableau.

## Claim Flag

```text
IF [Claim] = "Yes" THEN 1
ELSE 0
END
```

---

## Claim Rate

```text
AVG([Claim Flag])
```

Format the result as a percentage.

---

## Net Profit

For this exercise:

```text
[Net Sales] - [Commission (S$)]
```

Use the exact Commission field name in your dataset.

---

## Profit Margin

```text
IF [Net Sales] = 0 THEN 0
ELSE [Net Profit] / [Net Sales]
END
```

Format the result as a percentage.

---

# Analysis 1: Create the Executive KPIs

## Business Question

> **What is the overall performance of the travel insurance portfolio?**

Create KPI views for:

- Policies Sold
- Total Net Sales
- Total Net Profit
- Profit Margin
- Claim Rate

These metrics provide management with a quick overview of the business.

---

## Why Use KPIs?

An executive audience normally wants to understand the overall situation quickly.

KPIs help answer:

> **What is happening overall?**

For example:

```text
Policies Sold
63.3K
```

or:

```text
Claim Rate
1.5%
```

Keep each KPI simple and easy to read.

---

# Analysis 2: Net Sales by Product

## Business Question

> **Which travel insurance products contribute most to Net Sales?**

We need:

**Measure:** Net Sales

**Dimension:** Product Name

Recommended visual:

**Horizontal Bar Chart**

---

## Tableau Steps

1. Create a new worksheet.
2. Drag `Product Name` to Rows.
3. Drag `Net Sales` to Columns.
4. Confirm that Net Sales is aggregated using `SUM`.
5. Sort the products from highest to lowest.
6. Format the values so that they are easy to read.
7. Rename the worksheet:

`Net Sales by Product`

---

## What Should You Look For?

Ask:

- Which products contribute most to Net Sales?
- Is sales performance concentrated in only a few products?
- Are there products with noticeably lower sales?

The chart helps move from:

> **What is happening overall?**

to:

> **Where is performance coming from?**

---

# Analysis 3: Regional Performance

## Business Question

> **How does performance differ across regions?**

Use:

**Dimension:** Region

Start with:

**Measure:** Net Sales

Recommended visual:

**Bar Chart**

---

## Tableau Steps

1. Create a new worksheet.
2. Drag `Region` to Rows.
3. Drag `Net Sales` to Columns.
4. Sort from highest to lowest.
5. Rename the worksheet:

`Net Sales by Region`

---

# Add Claim Rate for Context

Net Sales alone does not tell us everything about performance.

Add:

`Claim Rate`

to the Tooltip.

This allows the user to see both:

- commercial performance, and
- claim risk.

For example, a region may generate high Net Sales but also have a higher Claim Rate.

This may indicate an area that deserves further investigation.

---

# Analysis 4: Compare Performance and Risk

Instead of creating many additional charts, we can enhance the regional analysis by comparing:

- Net Sales,
- Profit Margin,
- Claim Rate.

You may display these through:

- the tooltip,
- labels,
- an additional simple bar chart.

The purpose is to help answer:

> **Are the strongest commercial regions also associated with higher claim risk?**

Do not try to explain every possible driver at this stage.

The dashboard should help identify areas that may require further investigation.

---

# Step 5: Add a Filter

Add one useful filter to the analysis.

For example:

- Region,
- Channel,
- Product Name.

A filter should help the user answer a business question.

For example, selecting a Region may allow management to see:

> Which products contribute most to sales within this region?

Avoid adding too many filters.

# From Chart to Insight

Do not stop after creating the visual.

Ask:

> **What should the stakeholder notice?**

For example:

An observation might be:

> Region A has the highest Net Sales.

A stronger analytical statement might be:

> Region A contributes the largest share of Net Sales while maintaining a Claim Rate close to the overall portfolio average.

The second statement provides more business context.

# Key Takeaway

The goal of this exercise is not to create many different charts.

The goal is to create a **small number of useful visualisations that answer the most important business questions**.
