# Build an Executive Dashboard

In the previous exercise, you created the key worksheets needed to analyse the Travel Insurance portfolio.

You should now have:

- Executive KPIs
- Net Sales by Product
- Net Sales by Region
- Claim Rate information
- At least one useful filter

The final step is to combine these views into an **executive dashboard**.

---

# What is an Executive Dashboard?

An executive dashboard provides a high-level view of the most important business information.

It should allow management to quickly understand:

- overall performance,
- where performance is coming from,
- areas that may require attention.

The goal is not to show every analysis you have created.

Instead:

> **Show the most important information needed to understand the business situation.**

# Build Your Dashboard

Create a new dashboard in Tableau.

Add the worksheets you created in the previous exercise.

A simple structure could be:

```text
-----------------------------------------------------------------

             TRAVEL INSURANCE EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW

                       Region [Filter]

-----------------------------------------------------------------

 Policies      Net Sales      Net Profit      Profit Margin      Claim Rate

  XX.XK         $X.XXM         $X.XXM              XX%              X.X%

-----------------------------------------------------------------

                  NET SALES BY PRODUCT

                       [Bar Chart]

-----------------------------------------------------------------

                  NET SALES BY REGION

                       [Bar Chart]

-----------------------------------------------------------------
```

Your dashboard does not need to follow this layout exactly.

The important question is:

> **Can management understand the overall performance quickly?**

---

# Organise the Dashboard for the Audience

A useful reading flow is:

**Overall Performance  
→ Product Performance  
→ Regional Performance  
→ Area Requiring Attention**

Place the most important information near the top.

Keep the dashboard simple and avoid unnecessary charts, colours or filters.

---

# Add a Useful Filter

Add at least one filter that helps the user explore the dashboard.

For example:

- Region
- Channel
- Product

The filter should support a useful analytical question.

For example:

> **How does product performance change when a particular region is selected?**

Avoid adding too many filters.

---

# Communicate the Key Findings

Once the dashboard is complete, review the results and identify the most important findings.

Write **two or three key insights**.

For each insight, consider:

### What happened?

What does the data show?

### Why does it matter?

Why should management care?

### What should be investigated or considered next?

What action or further analysis may be useful?

---

# Example

An executive summary might follow this structure:

> **Overall:** The travel insurance portfolio shows strong overall sales performance.

> **Key Insight:** Sales are concentrated in a small number of products and regions, while Claim Rates vary across the portfolio.

> **Recommendation:** Management should investigate high-value segments with comparatively higher Claim Rates and identify strong-performing segments that may offer further opportunities.

This is only an example of the structure.

Your final conclusions should be based on your own analysis.

---

# Dashboard Checklist

Before completing the exercise, check:

## Content

- Are the most important KPIs included?
- Are only useful charts shown?
- Does the dashboard address the business problem?

## Design

- Is the dashboard easy to read?
- Is the most important information shown first?
- Is unnecessary clutter removed?

## Communication

- Can the audience identify the main findings quickly?
- Have you explained why the findings matter?
- Are your recommendations supported by the data?

# Final Takeaway

A good executive dashboard does not try to show everything.

It shows the **most important information for the audience** and helps answer:

> **What is happening, why does it matter, and where should we focus our attention?**
