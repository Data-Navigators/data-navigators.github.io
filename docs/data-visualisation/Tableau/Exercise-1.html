Analyse and Visualise the Data

You should now have a prepared version of the Travel Insurance dataset.

So far, we have:

  1. understood the business problem,
  2. translated it into analytical questions,
  3. reviewed and prepared the raw data.

We can now use Tableau to:

  1. create the required business metrics,
  2. analyse the data,
  3. build the key visualisations needed for the executive dashboard.

Remember:

Business Question → Metric + Dimension → Visualisation → Insight

The purpose is not to create as many charts as possible.

We will create only the views needed to answer the main business questions.

Step 1: Connect the Prepared Dataset

  1. Open Tableau Public.
  2. Select Text File or the appropriate file connection.
  3. Open:

travel_insurance_prepared.csv

  1. Review the fields and data types.
  2. Confirm that Tableau has correctly identified the dimensions and measures.

Review Dimensions and Measures

Your prepared dataset should contain dimensions such as:

  • Agency
  • Agency Type
  • Channel
  • Product Name
  • Claim
  • Destination
  • Region
  • Gender

Measures may include:

  • Duration
  • Net Sales
  • Commission
  • Age

Tableau may not always classify every field correctly.

Change a field between Dimension and Measure if necessary.

Step 2: Create the Business Metrics

Before building the visualisations, create the metrics required for the analysis.

Policies Sold​

Each row represents a travel insurance record.

Create a calculated field or use a record count to calculate policy volume.

For example:

COUNT([Product Name])

Name the field:

Policies Sold

Claim Flag​

The original Claim field contains:

  • Yes
  • No

Create a calculated field called:

Claim Flag

IF [Claim] = "Yes" THEN 1
ELSE 0
END

Claim Rate​

Create:

AVG([Claim Flag])

Name the field:

Claim Rate

Format the result as a percentage.

Because:

Claim = 1
No Claim = 0

the average represents the proportion of policy records with a claim.

Net Profit​

For this teaching exercise, create:

[Net Sales] - [Commission (S$)]

Name the field:

Net Profit

Use the exact Commission field name in your dataset.

Profit Margin​

Create:

IF [Net Sales] = 0 THEN 0
ELSE [Net Profit] / [Net Sales]
END

Name the field:

Profit Margin

Format the result as a percentage.

Step 3: Create the Executive KPIs

Business Question​

What is the overall performance of the travel insurance portfolio?

Create KPI worksheets for:

  • Policies Sold
  • Total Net Sales
  • Total Net Profit
  • Profit Margin
  • Claim Rate

These metrics will later appear at the top of the executive dashboard.

Creating a KPI

For example, to create:

Total Net Sales

  1. Create a new worksheet.
  2. Drag Net Sales to Text on the Marks card.
  3. Ensure the aggregation is SUM.
  4. Format the number so that it is easy to read.
  5. Rename the worksheet:

Total Net Sales

Repeat the process for the other KPIs.

Keep the KPI design simple.

An executive should be able to understand the number immediately.

Step 4: Analyse Product Performance

Business Question​

Which travel insurance products contribute most to Net Sales?

We need:

Measure: Net Sales

Dimension: Product Name

Recommended visual:

Horizontal Bar Chart

Tableau Steps​

  1. Create a new worksheet.
  2. Drag Product Name to Rows.
  3. Drag Net Sales to Columns.
  4. Confirm that Net Sales is aggregated using SUM.
  5. Sort the products from highest to lowest.
  6. Format the values so they are easy to read.
  7. Rename the worksheet:

Net Sales by Product

What Should You Look For?

Ask:

  • Which products contribute most to Net Sales?
  • Is sales performance concentrated in a small number of products?
  • Are there products with noticeably lower sales?

Do not stop at:

"Product A has the highest sales."

Ask:

"What should management notice about the product mix?"

Step 5: Analyse Regional Performance

Business Question​

Which regions contribute most to Net Sales?

We need:

Measure: Net Sales

Dimension: Region

Recommended visual:

Bar Chart

Tableau Steps​

  1. Create a new worksheet.
  2. Drag Region to Rows.
  3. Drag Net Sales to Columns.
  4. Confirm that Net Sales is aggregated using SUM.
  5. Sort the regions from highest to lowest.
  6. Rename the worksheet:

Net Sales by Region

Add Claim Rate for Context

Sales alone does not tell us everything about performance.

Add:

Claim Rate

to the Tooltip.

When a user moves over a region, they can now see:

  • Net Sales,
  • Claim Rate.

This helps answer:

Does a high-sales region also have a comparatively high Claim Rate?

The objective is not to perform a detailed risk investigation here.

The dashboard should simply help identify areas that may require closer attention.

Step 6: Add a Useful Filter

Add at least one useful filter.

For example:

  • Region
  • Channel
  • Product Name

A filter should help answer a business question.

For example:

Selecting a Region may help management understand:

Which products contribute most to Net Sales within this region?

Avoid adding too many filters.

An executive dashboard should remain simple.

What You Should Have Now

By the end of this exercise, you should have the following Tableau worksheets.

Executive KPIs​

  • Policies Sold
  • Total Net Sales
  • Total Net Profit
  • Profit Margin
  • Claim Rate

Supporting Visualisations​

  • Net Sales by Product
  • Net Sales by Region

Interactivity​

  • At least one useful filter

These views will form the basis of the executive dashboard in the next exercise.

From Visualisation to Insight

Creating the visualisation is only the first step.

Ask:

What should the stakeholder notice?

For example:

An observation might be:

Asia has the highest Net Sales.

A stronger analytical statement might be:

Asia contributes the largest share of Net Sales while its Claim Rate remains close to the overall portfolio average.

The second statement adds context and begins to explain why the result matters.

Your actual insight should always be based on what your data shows.

Keep the Analysis Focused

It is possible to create many more visualisations from this dataset.

For example, you could analyse:

  • agencies,
  • destinations,
  • age,
  • travel duration,
  • individual products,
  • additional claim patterns.

However, these are not required for this exercise.

Our goal is to build a simple executive overview, not a complete capstone analysis.

The selected views are sufficient to demonstrate:

Overall Performance
→ Product Performance
→ Regional Performance
→ Area Requiring Attention

Key Takeaway

The goal is not to create many charts.

The goal is to create a small number of useful visualisations that answer important business questions.

You should now have everything required to build the executive dashboard.

In the next section, we will combine these worksheets into a single executive overview and communicate the key findings.
